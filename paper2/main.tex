\begin{abstract}
A market maker quotes against a budget of capital, not contracts, yet its edge is reported per contract. On
Derive, a crypto options exchange, the margin rules are public smart contracts, and its off-chain engine quotes
margin for any book. Capital is what its three margin managers require for positions, not balances; it is
measured on one contract per fill of a companion paper and on the actual books of dominant maker
subaccounts. Four hypotheses were registered before any registered capital figure existed. Contrary to H1, capital
does not reorder the moneyness-by-tenor map of the edge in the registered sense (rank correlation 0.890); post hoc, much of that
correlation comes from the sign of the edge. In the books of the four subaccounts under the second-generation
portfolio manager (PM2), trading only ETH and HYPE, the median fill binds 3.5~per~cent of its stand-alone
capital (H2 not rejected). On the opening books of nine dominant subaccounts, standard margin needs 4.73 times
the PM2 capital (H3 not rejected), mostly for books too large for a standard-margin subaccount. Contrary to H4,
cheaper capital did not detectably narrow the half spread; the test has little power. The sample ends on
30~September 2026.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A market maker on an options venue does not run out of contracts. It runs out of capital. Funding constraints
act on the deposit that backs the margin of its positions \citep{brunnermeier2009}, and margin-based pricing
turns that deposit into a required return \citep{garleanu2011}. The literature on market making nonetheless
counts in contracts or their value. Inventory models penalise the position \citep{ho1981,avellaneda2008} or
bound it in units \citep{gueant2013}. Option market making prices the risk of the book
\citep{stoikov2009,garleanu2009}, the spread compensates for risks the maker cannot hedge
\citep{jameson1992}, and inventory risk moves option prices \citep{muravyev2016}. Even the illiquidity premium
in options is measured against the option price \citep{christoffersen2018}. Capital enters option prices
through the maker's wealth \citep{fournier2020} or aggregate intermediary constraints \citep{chen2019}. What a
unit of edge costs in capital remains open: outsiders can rarely combine an exact margin rule with the books
makers actually hold.

Derive, a crypto options exchange, makes both observable. It margins every subaccount under one of three
managers: a strategy-based standard manager (SM), a legacy portfolio manager (legacy PM) and a
second-generation portfolio manager (PM2). Each is a deterministic function of the positions, the price feeds
and risk parameters stored on chain, and its smart contracts can be queried at any past block. A public endpoint
evaluates any hypothetical book with the venue's off-chain trading engine. Makers' holdings are on chain too,
so their actual books can be priced. Capital is measured on positions under the venue's rules, not on account
balances, which mix what a book needs with what a trader deposits.

The question extends \citet{albiez2026}, which maps the maker's net edge over moneyness and tenor. Here the
same fills are priced in capital: how much capital does the edge bind, and does the map change when the edge
is measured per unit of capital? Option strategies lose much of their appeal once margin is charged
\citep{santaclara2009}; this paper takes the maker's side and uses the rule the venue actually applies.

The paper makes four contributions: a capital map over the moneyness and tenor grid of \citet{albiez2026}; the
marginal cost of a fill in the actual book of a dominant subaccount; the value of netting on the same books
under standard margin and PM2, in the tradition that compares strategy-based and portfolio margining on
identical positions \citep{kupiec1996}; and a price of capital. Makers' inventories and revenues move
liquidity \citep{comertonforde2010}, and margin changes move swaption prices \citep{ahn2025}; here, parameter
changes form a quasi-experiment: on-chain events that change the stand-alone capital of every cell by a known
amount.

Four hypotheses were registered before any capital figure of the registered analysis existed: that edge per
notional and edge per unit of PM2 capital rank the cells differently (H1); that a fill in the book of a
dominant subaccount under PM2 binds less than half its stand-alone capital per contract (H2); that PM2 saves
more than half the capital standard margin requires for the same books (H3); and that the half spread falls
when a parameter change makes capital cheaper (H4). Addenda~1 to~7 of the pre-registration are described
before the appendices.

H1 and H4 are rejected; H2 and H3 are not. The capital denominator does not reorder the map in the registered
sense: the rank correlation is 0.890, with an interval from 0.883 to 0.906 against a threshold of 0.5 (H1).
Post hoc, much of this correlation comes from the sign of the edge: among profitable cells it is 0.630 (0.585
to 0.700). In the books of the four subaccounts under PM2, which trade ETH and HYPE only, the median fill binds
3.5~per~cent of its stand-alone capital (H2); maker buys there mostly release capital, sells mostly bind it (post hoc).
Standard margin needs 4.73 times the PM2 capital for the opening books of nine dominant subaccounts (H3), most
of these books larger than a standard-margin subaccount accepts. I find no evidence that the half spread fell when
capital got cheaper (H4): the estimate has the wrong sign and a one-sided $p$ of 0.62, but the test cannot
exclude a sizeable narrowing.

\section{The engine and what it returns}\label{sec:engine}
This section derives the capital measure from the margin rules. The standard manager is strategy based. Each
option is margined in isolation, each expiry takes the smaller of its summed option margins and its maximum loss,
and expiries add up. A long option earns no credit of its own, so under SM a long book's capital is its full
premium. The legacy manager and PM2 are scenario based, in the spirit of Standard Portfolio Analysis of Risk
\citep{kupiec1994}: the book is revalued under spot and volatility shocks, and the requirement is the worst loss,
scaled by a margin factor, plus contingencies (panel c of Figures~\ref{fig:t1} and~\ref{fig:t2}). Both haircut
shocked expiry values with a static discount. PM2 adds skew scenarios, applies the haircut to positive and
negative expiry values alike, and discounts at a live rate feed; the legacy manager's rate stays at zero. Both net
within one underlying and never across, and the scope of netting decides what it saves
\citep{duffie2011,cont2014}. Probability-based margin goes back to \citet{figlewski1984}, and the worst-loss core
of scenario margin is a coherent risk measure \citep{artzner1999}. The managers' smart contracts are public
\citep{derivev2core}, with parameters on chain as in the lending protocols of \citet{qin2021}; BitMEX instead
margins each position at a percentage of its notional \citep{soska2021}.

The public endpoint \texttt{get\_margin} \citep{derivegetmargin} does not return a requirement. For positions
$q$ and USDC collateral $C$ it returns, for initial and for maintenance margin $X$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{net}_X(q;C) = C + V(q) - R_X(q),
\end{equation}
with $V$ the value of the positions in the manager's own valuation and $R_X$ the requirement. The venue
presumably admits a book whose initial net margin is non-negative; the chain enforces a weaker check, so
binding initial margin is an assumption. The fields \texttt{pre} and \texttt{post} evaluate the book as given
and after simulated changes at one price state within one request, which makes a marginal cost measurable.

Treating ${C - \mathrm{net}}$, which equals ${R - V}$, as the requirement would mix the value $V$ into the
margin. Before the registration, this gave ratios of standard to PM2 margin of 11.86 and 1.19 for the mixed probe books
of 17~September and 24~September 2026; on $R$ the ratios are 10.76 and 2.14 (Figure~\ref{fig:t2}). The second book's PM2 value of
$-496\,859$~USDC adds almost the same amount to numerator and denominator, pulling the ratio towards one. Netting
factors are properties of books, so this paper measures them on real ones.

The quantity used throughout is the capital needed to open a book $q$ at traded prices $p$ and meet initial
margin,
\begin{equation}
  K_p(q) = \sum_{i \in \text{options}} p_i\,q_i - \mathrm{net}_{\mathrm{IM}}(q;0).
\end{equation}
Collateral enters net margin one for one, so $K_p$ is the deposit at which the book is exactly admissible
once the premium has changed hands. The measure needs no valuation of its own: it differs from the
requirement $R_{\mathrm{IM}}$ by ${\sum p_i q_i - V}$, a valuation convention, not capital. For a trade
$\Delta q$ at prices $p$, one request with a collateral change of $-p\,\Delta q$ gives
${\Delta K = -(\mathrm{post} - \mathrm{pre})}$. Figure~\ref{fig:t1} maps $K_p$ of single contracts on the BTC
surface.

Two engines implement the managers. The off-chain engine behind the endpoint, on which the venue trades, prices at
fresh ticker marks and discounts PM2 at an undocumented flat rate; a box spread in each of the fourteen listed BTC
expiries on 24~September 2026 puts that rate at 2.0000~per~cent. The on-chain engine settles and liquidates, reads
feeds that lag the ticker, and discounts with the rate feed, which ranged from 3.64 to 3.82~per~cent across the
same expiries. Only the on-chain engine can be rebuilt for past blocks, so past fills are priced at their block with
offline replicas of its smart contracts. On 195 single contracts on 25~September 2026, the two
engines differed in PM2 capital by a median of 0.08~per~cent and at most 2.4~per~cent, at the longest tenor; whole
books, probed at random, showed larger gaps.

\section{Data and measurement}\label{sec:data}
The fills are those of \citet{albiez2026}: all 621\,508 BTC, ETH and HYPE option fills from 11~January 2024 to the
cut-off of 30~September 2026. Each carries the maker's side, price, size and net edge in USDC per contract: the
markout against the mark thirty~minutes after the fill, less fee net of rebate and a hedging cost. Fee and rebate,
booked per fill, are divided by its size (Addendum~2). On a multi-leg request for quote (RFQ) the
venue books the package fee on one leg, where it stays (Addendum~6). The companion paper was corrected on
25~September 2026 and uses the same unit per contract. SM covers the whole sample (HYPE from 11~November 2025; six
earlier HYPE fills have no manager), the legacy manager BTC and ETH throughout. The PM2 window is where
PM2 covers an underlying: BTC and ETH from 12~June 2025 23:00~UTC, HYPE from 11~November 2025. Only H4 uses
events before it.

A fill's market state is rebuilt from the parameter timelines and the last feed values pushed by its
block. The fill's stand-alone capital is that of a book holding only the fill, at its price, under every
manager available at the block: ${q = +1}$ for a maker buy and ${q = -1}$ for a sell (Figure~\ref{fig:f1}). A
cell combines an underlying, a maker side and one absolute delta and one tenor bucket of \citet{albiez2026}; a
cell with at least 200 fills is occupied. Its edge per unit of capital is a ratio of sums in basis points,
\begin{equation}
  e^{K}_c = 10^4\,\frac{\sum_{i \in c} \mathit{NE}_i\,a_i}{\sum_{i \in c} K_i\,a_i},
\end{equation}
with $\mathit{NE}$ the net edge and $a$ the size; its edge per unit of notional has the index price in place of
$K$.

The maker books are those of the ten subaccounts with the most maker fills, M1 to M10 by rank. Their opening books
are read on chain, with their manager, at the first block of each UTC day. Some share a wallet: M3 with M5, M4
with M10, and M2, the one SM subaccount, with M1, M6 and M8. The book before a fill is the opening book plus the
subaccount's balance changes up to the fill's transaction (Addendum~1). The marginal cost of a fill is
${\Delta K = K(\text{after}) - K(\text{before})}$ per contract under the subaccount's manager, relative to its
stand-alone capital.
The netting value of a maker-day is the SM capital of its opening book over its PM2 capital, each summed over
underlyings, with positions at the companion paper's mark.

For H4 each parameter change $j$ becomes a dose $d_{c,j}$: the mean of $\log(K^{+}/K^{-})$ over the fills of cell
$c$ in the fourteen~days before the event, where $K^{+}$ and $K^{-}$ are the capital of a fill after and before it
in the same market state. Doses are shown in log per cent, 100 times the log change; events whose largest absolute
dose is below one log per~cent are dropped. Over fourteen~days on either side of the event, excluding its day, the half
spread (the markout at the fill) in basis points of the index follows
\begin{equation}
  y_i = \alpha_{c,j} + \gamma_{t,u} + \beta\,\mathrm{post}_i\,d_{c,j} + \varepsilon_i,
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{post}_i$ marking fills after the event, $t$ the UTC day and $u$ the underlying; cells need 20
fills on each side.

Intervals are 90~per~cent percentile intervals of a cluster bootstrap over UTC days with 9\,999 draws
\citep{cameron2008}. H4 uses a wild cluster bootstrap with Rademacher weights and restricted residuals
\citep{mackinnon2017,roodman2019}. Its 100 placebo replications draw for each event a date at least 28~days from every change of the
underlying's legacy-manager parameters and default PM2 parameter set (subaccounts may carry their own;
Addendum~4). Replicas were validated
against the chain's evaluation (\texttt{eth\_call}) before any capital entered a test
(Appendix~\ref{app:validation}). Analyses not named in the registration are exploratory, those added after the
first results post hoc.

\subsection{The price of capital (H4)}\label{sec:h4}
Figure~\ref{fig:f5} places the parameter changes next to the capital of a fixed reference book, a short
straddle at the forward of each underlying (BTC in panel c). The registered set holds 18 events once same-day changes
are merged, without changes that touch only collateral parameters or the number of expiries
(Addendum~4); 14 pass the dose filter, and 13 have cells with at least 20 fills on each side, 475 cell-event
pairs: the H4 panel. The legacy-manager changes of 22~February 2025 for BTC and ETH, before the PM2
window, supply 75 of them. Of the 14 kept events, 11 made the reference book cheaper, by between 3.4 and
37.4~log per~cent across underlyings and between 7.8 and 26.0 for BTC. The largest BTC step, on 20~August 2026,
cut the PM2 capital of the straddle by 26.0~log per~cent (22.9~per~cent). Only the events of 8~January 2026
raised it, by about 1.6~log per~cent.

With its fixed effects, $\beta$ compares, within the same event, cells whose capital changed more with cells
whose capital changed less, mostly sells against buys (Figure~\ref{fig:f6}, panel a). It assumes that such
cells would have moved in parallel without the change and that makers did not anticipate it; the venue chose
the changes itself. The dose is the change in stand-alone capital, while in a dominant book a fill binds far
less (H2). The half spread is measured on fills, not quotes. At the kept events, the changed manager held
between 54.5 and 94.9~per~cent of the open interest.

The estimate of $\beta$ is $-4.60$~basis points of the index per unit of log capital, with a one-sided wild
cluster bootstrap $p$ of 0.6224, and 64~per~cent of the 100 placebo estimates are at least as large
(Figure~\ref{fig:f6}). H4 is rejected unless the estimate is positive with a one-sided $p$ of at most 0.05 and
lies above the 95th placebo percentile, here 23.41. The estimate fails both criteria, and H4 is rejected. The
sign is the opposite of the hypothesis, and with a cluster standard error of 13.09 the estimate cannot be told
apart from zero.

The test has little power. Its standard error holds the event dates fixed, and over the 100 placebo replications the
$t$ statistic has a standard deviation of 1.82 instead of one (post hoc). Calibrated on those placebo
statistics, the 90~per~cent range for $\beta$ runs from $-41.6$ to 29.7. For capital ten~per~cent cheaper,
the change in the half spread ranges from $-3.13$ to $+4.39$~basis points of the index, against a
panel mean of 7.93: a narrowing by up to 39~per~cent is not excluded. Only 141 day clusters carry the test,
and no variant in Appendix~\ref{app:inference} meets the registered rule.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
For a maker or a quoting bot on this venue the results have practical consequences. First, capital leaves the
order of the map largely intact (H1), but from an empty or small book the side matters more than the cell. A
long option far out of the money binds about its premium, and a short one binds scenario capital. The same
edge per notional is therefore a large edge per unit of capital when the maker buys a cheap wing and a small
one when it sells. The top cells per unit of capital owe their edge mostly to RFQ legs and to the SVI mark
extrapolated in the far wing (post hoc). Buys mostly release capital in the dominant PM2 books (post hoc), where
marginal capital orders the sides differently from stand-alone capital.

Second, the book, not the contract, decides the cost of a quote. In four large PM2 books the median fill binds
3.5~per~cent of its stand-alone capital (H2), so stand-alone capital (Figure~\ref{fig:f1}) overstates the
median cost of a quote many times over. The endpoint instead returns the marginal capital
against the maker's own book in one request.

Third, netting pays for books of a few dozen legs or more held under the portfolio managers (H3; by book size
exploratory). The one
standard-margin subaccount holds small books that net little; its operator keeps the large books under
portfolio margin.

Fourth, parameters are a risk the maker does not control: a single change cut the PM2 capital of the BTC
reference straddle by 22.9~per~cent, and the half spread showed no detectable response (H4). Capital may have
been slack, makers may have been bound by something else, or the test lacked power: for capital ten~per~cent
cheaper, a narrowing by up to 39~per~cent is not excluded (post hoc, Section~\ref{sec:h4}).

Several limits bound these readings. Capital is what the engine would require for the positions, not what makers
deposited. Initial margin is assumed to bind. Margin for open orders, charged by the venue but not returned by the
endpoint, lies outside the measure, as does every external bound on a book. Capital follows the on-chain engine,
while the venue trades on the off-chain one. The net edge deducts a hedging cost, while capital is that of the
unhedged contract. H2 rests on four PM2 subaccounts that trade ETH and HYPE only, H3 on books mostly larger than a
standard-margin subaccount accepts, and H4 on 13 events that mostly made capital cheaper. Collateral other than
USDC, held on most maker-days, is left outside $K$.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
On Derive the capital behind a quote is not a private number. The rules that set it are public smart contracts
with parameters on chain, so the capital of any book can be computed at any block, including books nobody held.
Measured that way, capital does not reorder the maker's map in the registered sense (H1): the rank correlation
is 0.890. Post hoc, much of that correlation comes from the sign of the edge, and the order changes more among
profitable cells and at the top of the map. The median fill in a dominant PM2 book binds 3.5~per~cent of its
stand-alone capital (H2). Netting is worth a factor of 4.73 over standard margin on the books that the dominant
subaccounts hold (H3), and most of these books are too large for a standard-margin subaccount. The half spread
showed no detectable response when parameter changes made capital cheaper (H4), but the test has little power.
Four hypotheses were registered before any capital figure of the registered analysis existed; H1 and H4 are
rejected, H2 and H3 are not. For a maker the capital that matters is that of the next fill in its own book, and
the off-chain engine quotes it on request.

\section{Post hoc and exploratory results}\label{app:inference}
Most of these analyses were added after the first results and are post hoc. The registration lists the maps
under other managers, per underlying and per unit of time as exploratory, and the placebo rule over every
timeline was computed with the results. None changes a verdict.

For H1, ranks shuffled within each sign group give a $\rho$ of 0.728 on average, with 5th and 95th percentiles
of 0.692 and 0.762. Among sells $\rho$ is 0.988 and among buys 0.705. In the groups by the sign of the edge
every replicate selects the cells by its own estimated edge; with the cells held fixed, the intervals would
ignore that selection. Of the ten best cells per unit of capital, none is among the ten best per notional; of
the best 20, five are. The percentile interval of the registered test is not centred on the estimate:
73~per~cent of the draws lie at or above it, and a bias-corrected interval runs from 0.871 to 0.898. Maps in the PM2
window under the other managers or per underlying give $\rho$ between 0.854 and 0.907; maps normalised by time
to expiry or by the empirical holding period give 0.780 and 0.803 (Figure~\ref{fig:f2}, panel c).
Without RFQ fills $\rho$ is 0.899 (0.889 to 0.913) over 157 cells. In the ten best cells per unit of PM2
capital, RFQ fills carry 93.4~per~cent of the edge with 39.3~per~cent of the fills.

For H2, the median runs from 0.0178 to 0.1172 over the parameter regimes R1 to R4, which the BTC changes of
Figure~\ref{fig:f1} bound. By maker side the median is 0.074 (0.062 to 0.094) for sells and $-0.128$ ($-0.202$ to
$-0.062$) for buys. Weighted by stand-alone capital, as the sum of $\Delta K$ over the sum of stand-alone capital
times size, all fills bind 0.137 of their stand-alone capital, sells 0.279, and buys release 0.464. Each leg of a
multi-leg RFQ package is measured against the book before the whole package (Addendum~1), without the other legs.
Collateral other than USDC, which the registration leaves outside $K$, is held on most maker-days. One PM2
subaccount holds ETH that its PM2 parameter set counts as offsetting risk, so counting that ETH would lower its
PM2 capital.

For H4 the estimate is $-3.42$ for BTC, $-1.90$ for ETH and $-10.12$ for HYPE. It is $-3.81$ on sell cells alone
and $-5.13$ with the dose weighted by the open-interest share of the changed manager. All of these estimates have
a one-sided $p$ above 0.5. On buy cells, whose doses are near zero, it is 128 with a $p$ of 0.13. These $p$ rest
on the day-cluster standard error, which the placebo dates show to be too small. A wider reading of Addendum~4
keeps placebo dates at least 28~days from every parameter change of the underlying under any manager, including SM
and the subaccounts' own PM2 parameter sets. It gives a 95th placebo percentile of 21.57, and 88~per~cent of the
placebo estimates are at least as large as $\beta$. The placebo panels hold one event more than the real panel,
since the HYPE event of 8~January 2026 has no cell there. The placebo dates of ETH under PM2 come from 10~days
between 16~July and 25~July 2025, so their windows overlap. With placebos only for events with cells and with
disjoint windows per underlying, the 95th percentile is 26.02, 48~per~cent of the placebo estimates are at least
as large, and the placebo $t$ has a standard deviation of 1.77. The dose is a mean of log ratios, and a few fills
near zero capital move it. With the median log ratio as dose the estimate is $-4.52$; without fills whose log
ratio exceeds one in absolute value, it is $-4.08$.
