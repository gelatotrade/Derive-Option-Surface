\begin{abstract}
A market maker quotes against a budget of capital, not contracts, yet its edge is reported per contract. On
Derive the margin rules are public contracts with parameters on chain, and the venue's off-chain engine can
be queried for any book. Under the venue's three margin managers, I measure capital by applying these rules to
hypothetical portfolios, not account balances: a single contract for each option fill of a companion paper
and the actual books of the dominant maker subaccounts. Capital does not reorder the moneyness-by-tenor map of
the edge in the registered sense (rank correlation 0.903; H1 rejected); post hoc, much of that correlation
comes from the sign of the edge. In the books of the four subaccounts under the second-generation portfolio
manager (PM2), which trade ETH and HYPE only, the median fill binds 3.5 per cent of its stand-alone capital.
On the opening books of nine dominant subaccounts, standard margin needs 4.75 times the PM2 capital, mostly for
books too large for a standard-margin account. I find no evidence that cheaper capital narrowed the half spread (H4
rejected). Four hypotheses were registered before any registered capital figure was computed; two are
rejected. Figures are from a pilot cut.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A market maker on an options venue does not run out of contracts. It runs out of capital. Funding constraints
act on the deposit that backs the margin of its positions \citep{brunnermeier2009}, and margin-based pricing
turns that deposit into a required return \citep{garleanu2011}. The literature on market making nonetheless
counts in contracts or their value. Inventory models penalise the position \citep{ho1981,avellaneda2008} or
bound it in units \citep{gueant2013}. Option market making prices the risk of the book
\citep{stoikov2009,garleanu2009}, the spread compensates for risks the maker cannot hedge
\citep{jameson1992}, and inventory risk moves option prices \citep{muravyev2016}. Even the illiquidity premium
in options is measured against the option price \citep{christoffersen2018}. Capital enters option prices
through the maker's wealth \citep{fournier2020} or aggregate intermediary constraints \citep{chen2019}. What a
unit of edge costs in capital remains open: outsiders can rarely combine an exact margin rule with the books
that market makers actually hold.

Derive makes both observable. The venue margins every subaccount under one of three managers: a strategy-based
standard manager (SM), a legacy portfolio manager (legacy PM) and a second-generation portfolio manager (PM2).
Each is a deterministic function of the positions, the price feeds and risk parameters stored on chain, and its
contracts can be called at any past block. A public endpoint evaluates any hypothetical book with the off-chain
engine on which the venue trades. The makers' holdings are also on chain, so their actual books can be priced
too. Capital is measured on portfolios under the venue's own rules, not on account balances, which mix what a
book needs with what a trader deposits.

The question extends \citet{albiez2026}, which maps the maker's net edge over moneyness and tenor. Here the
same fills are priced in capital: how much capital does the edge bind, and does the map change when the edge
is measured per unit of capital? Option strategies are known to lose much of their appeal once margin is
charged \citep{santaclara2009}; this paper takes the maker's side and uses the rule the venue actually applies.

The paper makes four contributions: a capital map, computed with the rules, over the moneyness and tenor
grid of \citet{albiez2026}; the marginal cost of a fill in the actual book of a dominant subaccount; the value of
netting on the same real books under standard margin and PM2, in the tradition that compares strategy-based
and portfolio margining on identical positions \citep{kupiec1996}; and a price of capital. Makers'
inventories and revenues are known to move liquidity \citep{comertonforde2010}, and margin changes move
swaption prices \citep{ahn2025}; here, parameter changes form a quasi-experiment: on-chain events that
change the stand-alone capital of every cell by a known amount.

Four hypotheses were registered before any registered capital figure was computed: that
edge per notional and edge per unit of PM2 capital rank the cells differently (H1); that a fill in the book of
a dominant subaccount under PM2 binds less than half of its stand-alone capital per contract (H2); that PM2
saves more than half of the capital standard margin requires for the same books (H3); and that the half
spread falls when a parameter change makes capital cheaper (H4).

Two of the four are rejected. The capital denominator does not reorder the map in the registered sense: the
rank correlation is 0.903, with an interval from 0.881 to 0.907 against a threshold of 0.5 (H1). Post hoc,
much of this correlation comes from the sign of the edge: among profitable cells it is 0.634 (0.572 to
0.702). In the books of the four subaccounts under PM2, which trade ETH and HYPE only, the median fill binds
3.5 per cent of its stand-alone capital (H2); maker buys there mostly release capital and maker sells mostly
bind it. Standard margin needs 4.75 times the PM2 capital for the opening books of nine dominant subaccounts
(H3), most of them larger than a standard-margin account accepts. I find no
evidence that the half spread fell when capital got cheaper (H4): the estimate has the wrong sign and a
one-sided $p$ of 0.62, but the test cannot exclude a sizeable narrowing.

\section{The engine and what it returns}\label{sec:engine}
The standard manager is strategy based. Each option is margined in isolation, each expiry takes the smaller of
its summed option margins and its maximum loss, and expiries are added up. A long option earns no credit of its
own, so under SM the capital of a long book is its full premium. The legacy manager and PM2 are scenario
engines in the spirit of SPAN \citep{kupiec1994}: the book is revalued under spot and volatility shocks, and
the requirement is the worst loss, scaled by a margin factor, plus contingencies. Both haircut shocked expiry
values with a static discount. PM2 adds skew scenarios, applies the haircut to positive and negative expiry
values alike, and discounts at a live rate feed; the legacy manager's rate stays at zero. Both net within one
underlying and never across, and the scope of netting decides what it saves \citep{duffie2011,cont2014}.
Probability-based margin goes back to \citet{figlewski1984}, and the worst-loss core of scenario margin is a
coherent risk measure \citep{artzner1999}. The managers' contracts are public \citep{derivev2core}, with
parameters on chain as in the lending protocols of \citet{qin2021}; BitMEX instead margins each position at a
percentage of its notional \citep{soska2021}.

The public endpoint \texttt{get\_margin} \citep{derivegetmargin} does not return a requirement. For positions
$q$ and USDC collateral $C$ it returns, for initial and for maintenance margin $X$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{net}_X(q;C) = C + V(q) - R_X(q),
\end{equation}
with $V$ the value of the positions in the manager's own valuation and $R_X$ the requirement. The venue
presumably admits a book when its initial net margin is not negative; on chain only a weaker check
is enforced, so a binding initial margin is an assumption. The fields \texttt{pre} and \texttt{post}
evaluate the book as given and after simulated changes, both at one price state within one request. That
makes a marginal cost measurable at one price state; the replica uses one block.

The quantity used throughout is the capital needed to open a book $q$ at traded prices $p$ and meet initial
margin,
\begin{equation}
  K_p(q) = \sum_{i \in \text{options}} p_i\,q_i - \mathrm{net}_{\mathrm{IM}}(q;0).
\end{equation}
Collateral enters net margin one for one, so $K_p$ is the deposit at which the book is exactly admissible
once the premium has changed hands. It needs no valuation of its own, and
$K_p - R_{\mathrm{IM}} = \sum p_i q_i - V$
is a valuation convention, not capital. For a trade $\Delta q$ at prices $p$, one request with a collateral
change of $-p\,\Delta q$ gives $\Delta K = -(\mathrm{post} - \mathrm{pre})$. Figure~\ref{fig:t1} maps $K_p$ of
single contracts on the BTC surface.

Two engines implement the managers. The off-chain engine behind the endpoint, on which the venue trades,
prices at fresh ticker marks and discounts PM2 at an undocumented flat rate; a box spread in each of the
fourteen listed BTC expiries on 24 September 2026 puts that rate at 2.0000 per cent. The on-chain engine
settles and liquidates, reads feeds that lag the ticker, and discounts with the rate feed, which ranged from
3.64 to 3.82 per cent across the same expiries. Only the on-chain semantics can be rebuilt for past blocks, so
the history uses it. On 195 single contracts on 25 September 2026, the two semantics differed in PM2 capital by a median
of 0.08 per cent and at most 2.4 per cent, at the longest tenor; whole books were only probed at random, with
larger gaps.

$C - \mathrm{net}$ is not the requirement: it equals $R - V$ and mixes the value of the positions into the
margin. In the probes that preceded this paper it gave ratios of standard to PM2 margin of 11.86 and 1.19 for
two books at blocks 44\,810\,149 and 45\,110\,142, a week apart; on $R$ the ratios are 10.76 and 2.14
(Figure~\ref{fig:t2}). The second book had a PM2 value of $-496\,859$ USDC, which adds almost the same amount
to numerator and denominator and pulls the ratio towards one. Netting factors are properties of books, so this
paper measures them on real ones.

\section{Data and measurement}\label{sec:data}
The fills are those of \citet{albiez2026}: every BTC, ETH and HYPE option fill from 11 January 2024 to the
pilot cut of 17 September 2026, 603\,940 in all. Each carries the maker's side, price and size, and the net
edge in USDC per contract thirty minutes after the fill. The venue reports the maker's fee and rebate as
totals per fill; here they are divided by the fill's size (Addendum 2). On a multi-leg RFQ the venue books
the fee of the whole package on one leg, where it stays (Addendum 6). The companion paper was corrected on
25 September 2026 and uses the same unit per contract. SM covers the whole sample (HYPE from 11 November 2025;
six earlier HYPE fills have no manager). The legacy manager covers BTC and ETH throughout. PM2 covers BTC and
ETH from 12 June 2025 23:00 UTC and HYPE from 11 November 2025; this span is the PM2 window of an underlying.
Only H4 also uses events before it.

A fill's market state is rebuilt from the parameter timelines and from the last feed values pushed up to and
including its block.

Capital per fill is that of a book holding only the fill, at the fill price and under every manager available
at the block: $q = +1$ when the maker bought and $q = -1$ when it sold. A cell combines an underlying, a maker
side and one absolute delta and one tenor bucket of \citet{albiez2026}; it is occupied from 200 fills. Its
edge per unit of capital is a ratio of sums in basis points,
\begin{equation}
  e^{K}_c = 10^4\,\frac{\sum_{i \in c} \mathit{NE}_i\,a_i}{\sum_{i \in c} K_i\,a_i},
\end{equation}
with $\mathit{NE}$ the net edge and $a$ the size; its edge per unit of notional has the index price in place of
$K$.

The maker books are those of the ten subaccounts with the most maker fills, labelled M1 to M10 by rank. Their
opening books are read on chain, with their manager, at the first block of each UTC day. Some share a wallet:
M3 with M5, M4 with M10, and M2, the one SM subaccount, with M1, M6 and M8. The book before a fill is the
opening book plus every balance change of the subaccount up to the fill's transaction (Addendum 1). The
marginal cost of a fill is $\Delta K = K(\text{after}) - K(\text{before})$ per contract under the account's
manager, relative to the stand-alone capital of the same contract. The netting value of a maker-day is the SM
capital of its opening book over its PM2 capital, each computed per underlying and then summed, with the book
priced at the companion paper's mark.

For H4 each parameter change $e$ becomes a dose $d_{c,e}$: the mean of $\log(K^{e+}/K^{e-})$ over the fills of
cell $c$ in the fourteen days before the event, where $K^{e+}$ and $K^{e-}$ are the capital of a fill after
and before it in the same market state. Events whose largest absolute dose is below one per cent are dropped.
Over fourteen days on either side of the event, without the event day, the half spread in basis points of the
index follows
\begin{equation}
  y_i = \alpha_{c,e} + \gamma_{t,u} + \beta\,\mathrm{post}_i\,d_{c,e} + \varepsilon_i,
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{post}_i$ marking fills after the event, $t$ the UTC day and $u$ the underlying; cells need 20
fills on each side.

Intervals are 90 per cent percentile intervals of a cluster bootstrap over UTC days with 9\,999 draws
\citep{cameron2008}. H4 uses a wild cluster bootstrap with Rademacher weights and restricted residuals
\citep{mackinnon2017,roodman2019}. It also uses 100 placebo dates, each at least 28 days from every change of
the underlying's legacy-manager and standard PM2 parameters (Addendum 4). The replicas were validated against
\texttt{eth\_call} before any capital entered a test (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}). Analyses not named in the
registration are exploratory, those added after the first results post hoc.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The capital denominator and the map (H1)}
Under PM2 a single maker sell binds between 7.8 and 17.7 per cent of notional in BTC, between 7.1 and 21.4 in
ETH and between 17.5 and 38.7 in HYPE (Figure~\ref{fig:f1}); a maker buy binds about its premium out of the
money and less in the money. Pooled over the window, PM2 is not the cheap manager for one short contract: the
ratio of standard margin to PM2 capital in the median sell cell is 0.943 in BTC, 0.902 in ETH and 0.875 in
HYPE. After the parameter change of 20 August 2026 the ratio is 1.398 for BTC and 1.140 for ETH, on the 6 and
18 sell cells that still reach 200 fills. The legacy manager needs more, with medians of 1.382 and 1.355 for BTC
and ETH sells.

In the PM2 window 173 cells have at least 200 fills, out of 210 with any fill (Figure~\ref{fig:f2}). Across
them, the rank correlation between edge per notional and edge per unit of PM2 capital is 0.903, with a 90 per
cent interval from 0.881 to 0.907. Since the upper bound exceeds 0.5, the registered rule
rejects H1.

\subsection{The price of capital (H4)}
Figure~\ref{fig:f5} places the parameter changes on the calendar next to the capital of a fixed reference
book, a short straddle at the forward of each underlying (BTC in panel c). The registered set holds 18 events
once changes of the same day are merged, without changes that touch only collateral parameters or the number
of expiries (Addendum 4); 14 pass the dose filter, and 13 have cells with at least 20 fills on each side, 475
cell-event pairs in all. The legacy-manager changes of 22 February 2025 for BTC and ETH, before the PM2
window, supply 75 of them. Of the 14 kept events, 11 made the reference book cheaper, by between 3.4 and 37.4
log per cent across underlyings and between 7.8 and 26.0 for BTC. The largest BTC step, on 20 August 2026,
cut the PM2 capital of the straddle by 22.9 per cent. Only the events of 8 January 2026 raised it, by about
1.6 log per cent.

With its fixed effects, $\beta$ compares, within the same event, cells whose capital changed more with cells
whose capital changed less, mostly sells against buys (Figure~\ref{fig:f6}, panel a). It assumes that such
cells would have moved in parallel without the change and that makers did not anticipate it; the venue chose
the changes itself. The dose is the change in stand-alone capital, while in a dominant book a fill binds far
less (H2). The half spread is measured on fills, not quotes. At the kept events, the changed manager held
between 54.5 and 94.9 per cent of the open interest.

The estimate of $\beta$ is $-4.60$ basis points of the index per unit of log capital, with a one-sided wild
cluster bootstrap $p$ of 0.6224, and 64 per cent of the 100 placebo estimates are at least as large
(Figure~\ref{fig:f6}). H4 is rejected unless the estimate is positive with a one-sided $p$ of at most 0.05 and
lies above the 95th placebo percentile, here 23.41. The estimate fails both criteria, and H4 is rejected. The
sign is the opposite of the hypothesis, and with a cluster standard error of 13.09 the estimate cannot be told
apart from zero.

The test has little power. Its standard error holds the event dates fixed, and at the 100 placebo dates the
$t$ statistic has a standard deviation of 1.82 instead of one (post hoc). Calibrated on those placebo
statistics, the 90 per cent range for $\beta$ runs from $-41.6$ to 29.7. For capital ten per cent cheaper,
the range of the change in the half spread runs from $-3.13$ to $+4.39$ basis points of the index, against a
panel mean of 7.93: a narrowing by up to 39 per cent is not excluded. Only 141 day clusters carry the test,
and no variant in Appendix~\ref{app:inference} meets the registered rule.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
For a maker or a quoting bot on this venue the results have practical consequences. First, from an empty or
small book, the side sets edge per unit of capital more than the cell does. A long option far out of the money
binds about its premium, and a short one binds scenario capital. The same edge per notional is therefore a
large edge per unit of capital when the maker buys a cheap wing and a small one when it sells. The top of the
capital map rests on the SVI mark in the wing and on RFQ legs. In the dominant PM2 books, buys mostly release
capital, so there the marginal capital orders the sides differently. The registered test finds no reordering
(H1). Second, the book, not the contract, decides the cost of a quote. In four large PM2 books the median fill
binds 3.5 per cent of its stand-alone capital (H2), so pricing a quote at the stand-alone capital of
Figure~\ref{fig:f1} overstates its median cost many times. The endpoint instead returns the marginal capital
against the maker's own book in one request. Third, netting pays for books of a few dozen legs or more held
under the portfolio managers (H3). The one standard-margin subaccount holds small books that net little; its operator
keeps the large books under portfolio margin. Fourth, parameters are a risk the maker does not control: a
single change cut the PM2 capital of the BTC reference straddle by 22.9 per cent, and the half spread showed no
detectable response (H4). Capital may have been slack, makers may have been bound by something else, or the
test lacks the power to detect a narrowing of that size.

Several limits bound these readings. Capital is measured on hypothetical portfolios, so it is what the engine
would require, not what makers deposited. Initial margin is assumed to bind. Margin for open orders, which the
venue charges but the endpoint does not return, lies outside the measure, and so does every external bound on
a book. The history uses the on-chain semantics, while the venue trades on the off-chain engine. The net edge
deducts a hedging cost, while capital is that of the unhedged contract. H2 rests on four PM2 accounts that
trade ETH and HYPE only, H3 on books mostly larger than a standard-margin account accepts,
and H4 on 13 events that mostly made capital cheaper. Collateral other than USDC, held on most maker-days, is
left outside $K$. All figures come from the pilot cut of 17 September 2026 and will be recomputed on the
registered sample.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
On Derive the capital behind a quote is not a private number. The rules that set it are public contracts with
parameters on chain, so the capital of any book can be computed at any block, including books nobody held.
Measured that way, capital does not reorder the maker's map in the registered sense: the rank correlation is
0.903. Post hoc, much of that correlation comes from the sign of the edge, and the order changes more among
profitable cells and at the top of the map. The median fill in a dominant PM2 book binds 3.5 per cent of its
stand-alone capital. Netting is worth a factor of 4.75 over standard margin on the books that the dominant
subaccounts hold, and most of these books are too large for a standard-margin account. The half spread showed
no detectable response when parameter changes made capital cheaper, but the test has little power. Four
hypotheses were registered before any capital figure of the registered analysis existed, and two are rejected. For a
maker the capital that matters is that of the next fill in its own book, and the off-chain engine quotes it on request.

\section{Post hoc and exploratory results}\label{app:inference}
Most of these analyses were added after the first results and are post hoc. The registration lists the maps
under other managers, per underlying and per unit of time as exploratory, and the placebo rule over every
timeline was computed with the results. None changes a verdict.

For H1, ranks shuffled within each sign group give a $\rho$ of 0.734 on average, with 5th and 95th percentiles
of 0.700 and 0.770. Among sells $\rho$ is 0.990 and among buys 0.721. In the groups by the sign of the edge
every replicate selects the cells by its own estimated edge; with the cells held fixed, the intervals would
ignore that selection. Of the ten best cells per unit of capital, one is among the ten best per notional; of
the best 20, five are. The percentile interval of the registered test is not centred on the estimate: 15 per
cent of the draws lie at or above it, and a bias-corrected interval runs from 0.898 to 0.920. Maps in the PM2
window under the other managers or per underlying give $\rho$ between 0.888 and 0.922; maps normalised by time
to expiry or by the empirical holding period give 0.806 and 0.833 (Figure~\ref{fig:f2}, panel c).
Without RFQ fills $\rho$ is 0.900 (0.889 to 0.913) over 153 cells. In the ten best cells per unit of PM2
capital, RFQ fills carry 89.6 per cent of the edge with 39.4 per cent of the fills.

For H2, the median runs from 0.0117 to 0.0899 over the parameter regimes R1 to R4, which the BTC changes of
Figure~\ref{fig:f1} bound. By maker side the median is 0.075 (0.063 to 0.092) for sells and $-0.137$
($-0.202$ to $-0.075$) for buys. Weighted by stand-alone capital, as the sum of $\Delta K$ over the sum of
stand-alone capital times size, all fills bind 0.166 of their stand-alone capital, sells 0.310, and buys
release 0.397. Each leg of a multi-leg RFQ package is measured against the book before the whole
package (Addendum 1), without the other legs. Collateral other than USDC, which the registration leaves outside
$K$, is held on most maker-days. One PM2 subaccount holds ETH that its library counts as offsetting risk, so
counting that ETH would lower its PM2 capital.

For H4 the estimate is $-3.42$ for BTC, $-1.90$ for ETH and $-10.12$ for HYPE. It is $-3.81$ on sell cells
alone and $-5.13$ with the dose weighted by the open-interest share of the changed manager. All of these
estimates have a one-sided $p$ above 0.5. On buy cells, whose doses are near zero, it is 128 with a $p$ of
0.13. These $p$ rest on the day-cluster standard error, which the placebo dates show to be too small. A wider
reading of Addendum 4 keeps placebo dates at least 28 days from every parameter change of the underlying under
any manager, including SM and the account libraries. It gives a 95th placebo percentile of 21.57, and 88 per
cent of the placebo estimates are at least as large as $\beta$. The placebo panels hold one event more than
the real panel, since the HYPE event of 8 January 2026 has no cell there. The placebo dates of ETH under PM2
come from 10 days between 16 July and 25 July 2025, so their windows overlap. With placebos only for events
with cells and with disjoint windows per underlying, the 95th percentile is 26.02, 48 per cent of the placebo
estimates are at least as large, and the placebo $t$ has a standard deviation of 1.77. The dose is a mean of
log ratios, and a few fills near zero capital move it. With the median log ratio as dose the estimate is
$-4.52$; without fills whose log ratio exceeds one in absolute value, it is $-4.08$.
