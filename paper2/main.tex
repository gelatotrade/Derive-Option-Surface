\begin{abstract}
A market maker quotes against a budget of capital, not of contracts, yet its edge is reported per contract. On
Derive the margin engine that turns a book into required capital is public, with parameters stored on chain. I
measure capital by evaluating that engine on hypothetical portfolios, not on account balances: a single
contract for each option fill of a companion paper and the actual books of the dominant makers, under the
venue's three margin managers. Capital does not reorder the moneyness-by-tenor map of the edge in the
registered sense (rank correlation 0.903; H1 rejected), but much of that correlation is the sign of the edge;
among profitable cells the two rankings differ markedly. In the books of the four makers under the newer
portfolio manager (ETH and HYPE only), a fill binds a median 3.5 per cent of its stand-alone capital per
contract. Standard margin needs 4.75 times that manager's capital for the same books, mostly books no
standard-margin account could hold. I find no evidence that cheaper capital narrowed the half spread (H4
rejected). Four hypotheses were registered before any capital figure of the registered analysis was computed;
two are rejected. Figures are from a pilot cut.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A market maker on an options venue does not run out of contracts. It runs out of capital. The deposit behind
the margin on its positions is the input on which funding constraints act \citep{brunnermeier2009} and which
margin-based pricing turns into a required return \citep{garleanu2011}. The literature on market making
nonetheless counts in contracts. Inventory models bound the position in units
\citep{ho1981,avellaneda2008,gueant2013}, option market making prices the risk of the book
\citep{stoikov2009,garleanu2009}, the option spread compensates for risks the maker cannot hedge
\citep{jameson1992}, and inventory risk moves option prices \citep{muravyev2016}. Even the illiquidity premium
in options is measured against the price of the option \citep{christoffersen2018}. What a unit of edge costs
in capital is left open, because outsiders can rarely combine an exact margin rule with the books that market
makers actually hold.

Derive makes both observable. The venue margins every subaccount under one of three managers: a strategy-based
standard manager (SM), a legacy portfolio manager and a second-generation portfolio manager (PM2). Each is a
deterministic function of the positions, the price feeds and risk parameters stored on chain, and its
contracts can be called at any past block. A public endpoint evaluates the off-chain engine the venue trades
on for any hypothetical book today. The makers' holdings are on chain as well, so the capital of the books
they actually held can be computed too. I measure capital on portfolios evaluated by the venue's own rules,
not on account balances that mix what a book needs with whatever a trader chooses to deposit.

The question is the one \citet{albiez2026} leaves open. That paper maps the maker's net edge over moneyness
and tenor. Here the same fills are priced in capital: how much capital does the edge bind, and does the map
change when the edge is measured per unit of capital? Option strategies are known to lose much of their appeal
once margin is charged \citep{santaclara2009}; this paper takes the maker's side and uses the rule the venue
actually applies.

Four contributions follow: a capital map over the moneyness-by-tenor grid of \citet{albiez2026}, computed with
the engine itself; the marginal cost of a fill in the actual book of a dominant maker; the value of netting on
the same real books under standard margin and PM2, in the tradition that compares strategy-based and portfolio
margining on identical positions \citep{kupiec1996}; and a price of capital. That makers' balance sheets move
liquidity is known from their inventories and revenues \citep{comertonforde2010}; here, parameter changes are
on-chain events that change the stand-alone capital of every cell by a known amount, a quasi-experiment.

Four hypotheses were registered before the first capital figure of the registered analysis was computed: that
edge per notional and edge per unit of PM2 capital rank the cells differently (H1); that a fill in the book of
a dominant maker under PM2 binds less than half of its stand-alone capital per contract (H2); that PM2 saves
more than half of the capital standard margin requires for the same books (H3); and that the half spread falls
when a parameter change makes capital cheaper (H4).

Two of the four are rejected. The capital denominator does not reorder the map in the registered sense: the
rank correlation is 0.903, with an interval from 0.881 to 0.907 against a threshold of 0.5 (H1). Much of it is
the sign of the edge, which a positive denominator keeps; among profitable cells the correlation is 0.634. A
fill is cheap at the margin: in the books of the four makers under PM2, which trade ETH and HYPE only, its
median marginal capital is 3.5 per cent of its stand-alone capital (H2). Standard margin needs 4.75 times the
PM2 capital of the same books (H3), mostly books larger than a standard-margin account accepts, while the one
maker under standard margin would save little. I find no evidence that the half spread fell when capital got
cheaper: the estimate has the wrong sign and a one-sided $p$ of 0.62, but its interval does not exclude a
narrowing (H4).

\section{The engine and what it returns}\label{sec:engine}
The standard manager is strategy based. Each option is margined in isolation, each expiry takes the smaller of
that sum and its maximum loss, and expiries are added up. A long option earns no credit of its own, so under
SM the capital of a long book is its full premium. The legacy manager and PM2 are scenario engines in the
spirit of SPAN \citep{kupiec1994}: the book is revalued under spot and volatility shocks, and the worst loss,
scaled by a margin factor, plus contingencies is the requirement. Both haircut shocked expiry values with a
static discount; PM2 adds skew scenarios, applies it to positive and negative expiry values alike, and
discounts at a live rate feed, where the legacy manager's rate stays at zero. Both net within one underlying
and never across, and the scope of netting decides what it saves \citep{duffie2011,cont2014}.
Probability-based margin goes back to \citet{figlewski1984}, and the worst-loss core of scenario margin is a
coherent risk measure \citep{artzner1999}. The rule is public code with public parameters, as in the lending
protocols of \citet{qin2021}, not an engine only the operator of a centralised venue can run
\citep{soska2021}.

The public endpoint \texttt{get\_margin} does not return a requirement. For positions $q$ and USDC
collateral $C$ it returns, for initial and for maintenance margin $X$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{net}_X(q;C) = C + V(q) - R_X(q),
\end{equation}
with $V$ the value of the positions in the manager's own valuation and $R_X$ the requirement. The venue
presumably admits a book when its initial net margin is not negative; on chain only a weaker check
is enforced, so a binding initial margin is an assumption. The field \texttt{pre}
evaluates the book as given and \texttt{post} the book after simulated changes, both at one price state
within one request. That makes a marginal cost measurable, as two requests would also compare two prices; for the
history, the replica evaluates both books at one block.

The quantity used throughout is the capital needed to open a book $q$ at traded prices $p$ and meet initial
margin,
\begin{equation}
  K_p(q) = \sum_{i \in \text{options}} p_i\,q_i - \mathrm{net}_{\mathrm{IM}}(q;0).
\end{equation}
Collateral enters net margin one for one, so $K_p$ is the deposit at which the book is exactly admissible
once the premium has changed hands. It needs no valuation of its own, and
$K_p - R_{\mathrm{IM}} = \sum p_i q_i - V$
is a valuation convention, not capital. For a trade $\Delta q$ at prices $p$, one request with a collateral
change of $-p\,\Delta q$ gives $\Delta K = -(\mathrm{post} - \mathrm{pre})$. Figure~\ref{fig:t1} maps $K_p$ of
single contracts on the BTC surface.

Two engines implement the managers. The off-chain engine behind the endpoint, on which the venue trades,
prices at fresh ticker marks and discounts PM2 at an undocumented flat rate, which a box spread in each of the
fourteen listed BTC expiries on 24 September 2026 puts at 2.0000 per cent. The on-chain engine settles and
liquidates, reads feeds that lag the ticker, and discounts with the rate feed, which ranged from 3.64 to 3.82
per cent across the same expiries. Only the on-chain semantics can be rebuilt for past blocks, and the history
uses it. On 195 single contracts on 25 September 2026, the two semantics differed in PM2 capital by a median
of 0.08 per cent and at most 2.4 per cent, at the longest tenor.

$C - \mathrm{net}$ is not the requirement: it equals $R - V$ and mixes the value of the positions into the
margin. In the probes that preceded this paper it gave ratios of standard to PM2 margin of 11.86 and 1.19 for
two books at blocks 44\,810\,149 and 45\,110\,142, a week apart; on $R$ the ratios are 10.76 and 2.14
(Figure~\ref{fig:t2}). The second book had a PM2 value of $-496\,859$ USDC, which adds almost the same amount
to numerator and denominator and pulls the ratio towards one. Netting factors are properties of books, which
is why this paper measures them on real ones.

\section{Data and measurement}\label{sec:data}
The fills are those of \citet{albiez2026}: every BTC, ETH and HYPE option fill from 11 January 2024 to the
pilot cut of 17 September 2026, 603\,940 in all, each with the maker's side, price, size and the net edge
thirty minutes after the fill in USDC per contract; the registered sample runs to 30 September 2026. The venue
reports the maker's fee and rebate as totals per fill, so here they are divided by the size of the fill
(Addendum 2), which matters because every cell value weights the edge by size. The registration took the net
edge from the companion paper, which subtracts the totals from the markout per contract; that form is kept as
a sensitivity. SM covers the whole sample (HYPE from 11 November 2025; six earlier HYPE fills have no
manager), the legacy manager BTC and ETH throughout, and PM2 BTC and ETH from 12 June 2025 23:00 UTC and HYPE
from 11 November 2025. That span is the PM2 window of an underlying, and every test runs inside it.

The market state of a fill is rebuilt from the feed values last pushed before its block, and parameters at
every block where they changed (Figure~\ref{fig:f5}).

Capital per fill is that of a book holding only the fill: $q = +1$ when the maker bought and $q = -1$ when
it sold, at the fill price, under every manager available at the block. A cell is an underlying, a maker
side, an absolute delta bucket and a tenor bucket from \citet{albiez2026}, occupied from 200 fills. Its edge per unit of capital and per unit of
notional are ratios of sums in basis points,
\begin{equation}
  e^{K}_c = 10^4\,\frac{\sum_{i \in c} NE_i\,a_i}{\sum_{i \in c} K_i\,a_i}, \qquad
  e^{N}_c = 10^4\,\frac{\sum_{i \in c} NE_i\,a_i}{\sum_{i \in c} S_i\,a_i},
\end{equation}
with $NE$ the net edge, $a$ the size and $S$ the index price. Edge is a flow per fill and capital a stock, so
$e^{K}_c$ is not a return per unit of time. The net edge deducts a
modelled cost of delta hedging, while $K$ is the capital of the unhedged contract.

The maker books are those of the ten subaccounts with the most maker fills, read on chain with their manager
at the first block of each UTC day. The book before a fill adds every balance change of the subaccount up to
the fill's transaction (Addendum 1). The marginal cost of a fill is $\Delta K = K(\text{after}) -
K(\text{before})$ per contract under the account's manager, relative to the stand-alone capital of the same
contract. The netting value of a maker-day is the capital of its opening book under SM over that under PM2,
each underlying computed separately and summed.

For H4 each parameter change becomes a dose: $d_{c,e}$ is the mean of $\log(K_{\text{after}} /
K_{\text{before}})$ over the fills of cell $c$ in the fourteen days before event $e$, both capitals pricing
the same fill in the same market state. Events whose largest absolute dose is below one per cent are dropped.
Over fourteen days on either side of the event, without the event day, the half spread in basis points of the
index follows $y_i = \alpha_{c,e} + \gamma_{t,u} + \beta\,\mathrm{post}_i\,d_{c,e} + \varepsilon_i$, with $t$
the UTC day and $u$ the underlying; cells need 20 fills on each side.

Intervals are 90 per cent percentile intervals of a cluster bootstrap over UTC days with 9\,999 draws. H4 uses
a wild cluster bootstrap with Rademacher weights and restricted residuals, and 100 placebo dates at least 28
days from any parameter change of the underlying. Before any capital entered a test, the offline replicas were
validated against \texttt{eth\_call} on 100 random blocks per underlying and manager (registered: at least 48)
and on 20 maker-days; all 32 cells meet the registered thresholds (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}).

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The capital denominator and the map (H1)}
Under PM2 a single maker sell binds (Figure~\ref{fig:f1}) between 7.8 and 17.7 per cent of notional in BTC,
between 7.1 and 21.4 in ETH and between 17.5 and 38.7 in HYPE; a maker buy binds about its premium. Pooled
over the window, PM2 is not the cheap manager for one short contract: the ratio of standard margin to PM2
capital in the median sell cell is 0.943 in BTC, 0.902 in ETH and 0.875 in HYPE. After the parameter change of
20 August 2026 it is 1.398 and 1.140 for BTC and ETH. The legacy manager needs more, with medians of 1.382 and
1.355 for BTC and ETH sells.

Across the 173 cells with at least 200 fills in the PM2 window, out of 210 with any fill
(Figure~\ref{fig:f2}), the rank correlation between edge per notional and edge per unit of PM2 capital is
0.903, with a 90 per cent interval from 0.881 to 0.907. Since the upper bound exceeds 0.5, the registered rule
rejects H1.

The rejection says less than its margin suggests. Capital is positive in every cell, so a cell keeps the sign
of its edge in both maps, and the 99 profitable cells rank first in both. Shuffling ranks within each sign
group alone gives a $\rho$ of 0.734 (5th to 95th percentile 0.700 to 0.770), and among the profitable cells
alone $\rho$ is 0.634. The registered threshold of 0.5 did not allow for this structure. Among sells the order
barely changes ($\rho$ of 0.990); among buys it changes more (0.721), and among profitable buys little of it
survives (0.243). Of the ten best cells per unit of capital, one is among the ten best per notional; of the
best 20, five are.

Pooled, the net edge is 3.38 basis points of notional and 39.9 of PM2
capital. The median cell moves by 14 ranks. The largest moves are maker buys of options far out of the money:
a long option binds only its premium, so a thin edge per notional becomes a large edge per unit of capital.
BTC buys in the lowest delta bucket with two to seven days to expiry rise from rank 76 to 4; the same buys
with at most two days fall from rank 121 to 173, the last. Maker sells, which bind scenario capital, move
less, and none ranks higher than 53rd per unit of capital. The 20 fills with a PM2 capital that is not
positive stay in the sums (Addendum 4).

\subsection{A fill in a maker's book (H2)}
Of the ten dominant subaccounts, four were margined under PM2 at the start of a day in the window (Addendum
1); they trade ETH and HYPE only. Their 100\,995 fills exceed 20\,000, so the registered random sample of
20\,000 applies; after the registered exclusion of one fill with a stand-alone capital that is not positive,
19\,999 fills on 372 days remain. Figure~\ref{fig:f3} shows the distribution of $\Delta K /
K_{\text{single}}$, the marginal capital of the whole fill per contract. Its median is 0.0345, with a 90 per
cent interval from 0.0307 to 0.0386, and 42.0 per cent of the fills leave the capital of the book unchanged or
reduce it. H2 is not rejected: in these books the median fill costs 3.5 per cent of what it would cost alone.

The median hides a wide spread. About a quarter of the fills free more than 0.28 of their stand-alone capital,
a quarter bind more than 0.63 of it, and from the 95th percentile on a fill binds about its full stand-alone
capital and almost never more. A fill against the scenario that binds the book releases capital, and a fill
with it costs close to what it costs alone, so the median mixes fills that offset the book's risk with fills
that add to it. Per account the median runs from 0.0230 for the single HYPE account to 0.0505, and per
parameter regime from 0.0117 to 0.0899.

\subsection{What netting is worth (H3)}
Figure~\ref{fig:f4} reports the capital of the same opening books under SM and under PM2 over 1\,943
maker-days of nine subaccounts in the PM2 window with at least one option position: four under PM2, four under
the legacy manager, for which both capitals are counterfactual, and one under SM; the tenth made no maker fill
in the window. None has a PM2 capital that is not positive. The median of $K_{\mathrm{SM}}/K_{\mathrm{PM2}}$
is 4.747, with a 90 per cent interval from 4.662 to 4.824; clusters are days, not accounts, and eight of the

manager needs 1.545 times the PM2 capital, a comparison without a threshold, and standard margin 3.245 times

the book holds more than 63 options, the most the endpoint accepted for a standard-margin account in probes of

\subsection{The price of capital (H4)}
Figure~\ref{fig:f5} places the parameter changes on the calendar next to the capital of a fixed reference
book, a short BTC straddle at the forward. The registered set holds 18
events once changes of the same day are merged, without changes to collateral parameters or the number of
expiries only (Addendum 4); 14 pass the dose filter, and 13 have cells with at least 20 fills on each side,
475 cell-event pairs in all. Almost every kept event made the reference book cheaper, by between 3.4 and 37.4
log per cent; for BTC the largest step came on 20 August 2026, when the PM2 capital of the straddle fell by
22.9 per cent (26.0 log per cent). Only the events of 8 January 2026 raised it, by about 1.6 log per cent.

With its fixed effects, $\beta$ compares cells whose capital changed more with cells whose capital changed
less within the same event, mostly sells against buys (Figure~\ref{fig:f6}, panel a). It assumes that such
cells would have moved in parallel without the change and that makers did not anticipate it; the venue chose
the changes itself. The dose is the change in stand-alone capital, while in a dominant book a fill binds far
less (H2); the half spread is measured on fills, not quotes; and the changed manager held between 54.5 and
94.9 per cent of the open interest at the kept events.

The estimate of $\beta$ is $-4.60$ basis points of the index per unit of log capital, with a one-sided wild
cluster bootstrap $p$ of 0.6224, and 64 per cent of the 100 placebo estimates are at least as large
(Figure~\ref{fig:f6}). H4 is rejected unless the estimate is positive with a one-sided $p$ of at most 0.05 and
above the 95th placebo percentile, here 23.41; it fails both criteria and is rejected. The sign is the
opposite of the hypothesis, and with a cluster standard error of 13.09 the estimate cannot be told apart from
zero. For capital ten per cent cheaper, the descriptive 90 per cent interval spans a change in the half spread
from $-1.75$ to $+2.74$ basis points of the index, against a mean half spread of 7.93 in the panel, so a
narrowing by a fifth is not excluded. Per underlying the estimate is $-3.42$ for BTC, $-1.90$ for ETH and
$-10.12$ for HYPE, on sell cells alone $-3.81$, and with the dose weighted by the open-interest share $-5.13$, each with a one-sided $p$ above 0.5;
on buy cells, whose doses are near zero, it is 128 with a $p$ of 0.13. The support is thin: 141 day clusters, and the placebo dates of ETH under
PM2 can be drawn from 10 days only, between 16 July and 25 July 2025.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
For a maker or a quoting bot on this venue the results have practical consequences. First, edge per unit of
capital is set by the side more than by the cell: a long option binds its premium and a short one scenario
capital, so the same edge per notional is a large edge per unit of capital when the maker buys a cheap wing
and a small one when it sells. Among sells the order of the cells barely changes; among buys, where capital is
the premium, it changes more, among profitable buys a great deal, and so does the top of the map (H1). Second, the cost of a quote is
decided by the book, not by the contract. In four large PM2 books the median fill binds 3.5 per cent of its
stand-alone capital (H2), so pricing a quote at the stand-alone capital of Figure~\ref{fig:f1} overstates the
cost of the median fill many times; in the off-chain semantics the endpoint returns the marginal capital
against the maker's own book in one request. Third, netting pays for the books that makers hold under the
portfolio managers from a few dozen legs on, while the one maker under standard margin holds books that net
little (H3). For one short contract PM2 needed more than standard margin in the median sell cell pooled over
the window, but less after 20 August 2026. Fourth, parameters are a risk the maker does not control: a single
change cut the PM2 capital of the BTC reference straddle by 22.9 per cent, and the half spread showed no
measurable response to such changes (H4); whether capital was slack or makers were bound by something else,
these data cannot tell.

Several limits bound these readings. Capital is measured on hypothetical portfolios, so it is the capital the
engine would require and not what makers deposited, and initial margin is assumed to be what binds. Margin for
open orders, which the venue charges but the endpoint does not return, lies outside the measure, and so does
every external bound on a book. The history is in chain semantics, while the venue trades on the off-chain
engine. The net edge deducts a hedging cost, while capital is that of the unhedged contract. H2 rests on four
PM2 accounts that trade ETH and HYPE only, H3 on books mostly larger than a standard-margin account accepts,
and H4 on 13 events that mostly made capital cheaper. Collateral other than USDC is left outside $K$, and its
share is not measured. All figures come from the pilot cut of 17 September 2026 and will be recomputed on the
registered sample.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
On Derive the capital behind a quote is not a private number. The engine that sets it is public code with
parameters on chain, so the capital of any book can be computed at any block, including books nobody held.
Measured that way, capital does not reorder the maker's map in the registered sense, with a rank correlation
of 0.903, but much of that correlation is the sign of the edge; among profitable cells and at the top of the
map the order changes. Capital is cheap at the margin, where the median fill in a dominant PM2 book binds 3.5
per cent of its stand-alone capital. Netting is worth a factor of 4.75 over standard margin on the books the
dominant makers hold, books mostly too large for a standard-margin account. The half spread showed no
measurable response when parameter changes made capital cheaper. Four hypotheses were registered before any
capital figure of the registered analysis existed, and two are rejected. For a maker the capital that matters
is that of the next fill in its own book, and the off-chain engine quotes it on request.
